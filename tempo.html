What a preset visualiser can't do

Both panels get identical audio from the same analyser. The left one reacts to the amplitude envelope — which is all a MilkDrop-family visualiser has: bass, mid, treble, and nothing else. The right runs on a tracked period and phase, so it knows where the next beat lands before it arrives.

The test clip has six seconds with no percussion whatsoever. Watch the left panel go inert while the right keeps time.

All audio here is generated — drums, bass and guitar synthesised at a known tempo. On real commercial recordings the detector leans considerably harder on each song's reference tempo, because mastering compresses away the transients onset detection depends on. The confidence bar is honest about it: a bad lock reads low.
